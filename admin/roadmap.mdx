# Build a public roadmap

Show users what you're building. A public roadmap builds trust. Users can see what's planned, in progress, and complete, and follow updates on features they care about.

## How it works

A roadmap is a saved view, not a list you curate by hand. You define a **base filter** (which boards, tags, statuses, or [segments](/admin/segments) qualify) and a set of **columns**. Any post that matches the filter shows up in the matching column automatically. There's no "add to roadmap" step.

<Note>
Posts appear and disappear from a roadmap only because their status, board, tags, or segment changed, not because someone added or removed them. Deleting a roadmap never touches the underlying posts.
</Note>

## Create a roadmap

You can have multiple roadmaps for different purposes (e.g., "Product Roadmap", "API Roadmap").

1. Go to **Admin → Roadmap**
2. Click the **+** button in the sidebar
3. Fill in the details:
   - **Name** - Display name (e.g., "Product Roadmap")
   - **Layout** - Status columns or date periods (see below)
   - **Description** - Optional description
   - **Visibility** - Public, team only, or customer segments
4. Set the **base filter** - which statuses, boards, tags, or segments a post must match to appear at all
5. For a status-columns roadmap, choose which statuses become columns
6. Click **Create**

## Choose a layout

Each roadmap uses one of two layouts:

**Status columns** groups posts by [status](/admin/statuses), kanban-style. Each column maps to one status. When creating a new roadmap, columns are pre-filled from statuses with "Show on Roadmap" enabled, but you can add, remove, rename, recolor, and reorder columns for that roadmap independently.

**Date periods** groups posts by their target ship date (`eta`) into monthly, quarterly, or semiannual buckets, plus a "No ETA" bucket for posts without one. Set the eta on a post's detail page, or by dragging its card between buckets.

<Tip>
Use status columns for most roadmaps. Date periods work well when your team commits to ship dates and wants users to see a timeline instead of a workflow stage.
</Tip>

## Move posts between columns

Drag and drop still works, but it changes the post's underlying field instead of a roadmap-only placement:

- On a **status columns** roadmap, dragging a card to another column changes the post's status.
- On a **date periods** roadmap, dragging a card to another bucket sets (or clears) the post's eta.

Either way, the change is visible everywhere that status or eta appears, including [Feedback](/admin/inbox) and the public roadmap.

<Tip>
This is the fastest way to update multiple posts at once. Great for sprint planning sessions.
</Tip>

## Roadmap visibility

Each roadmap has one of three visibility settings:

| Setting | Who can see it |
|---------|----------------|
| **Public** | Everyone visiting your portal |
| **Team only** | Only team members (indicated by a lock icon in the sidebar) |
| **Customer segments** | Only visitors in the [segments](/admin/segments) you select |

<Tip>
Use a team-only roadmap for internal planning, then move items to your public roadmap when you're ready to announce them by adjusting the base filter or visibility.
</Tip>

## What users see

On your public roadmap, users can see:
- Post title
- Vote count
- Board
- Target date, if the post has an eta

Users do **NOT** see:
- Internal notes
- Team comments
- Assignment details

## Best Practices

### Keep It Updated

<Warning>
An outdated roadmap undermines trust. Regularly:
- Move completed items to your "Complete" status
- Refine your base filter so newly planned items qualify automatically
- Retire statuses or tags that no longer belong on the roadmap
</Warning>

### Show Meaningful Items Only

Scope your base filter so the roadmap doesn't fill with minor tasks. Focus on:
- Major features
- Popular requests (high vote count)
- Significant improvements

### Avoid Specific Dates

<Tip>
Unless you're certain, avoid committing to dates. Instead use:
- Status columns for most roadmaps
- Date periods only when you're confident in the eta
- A "No ETA" bucket for ideas still under consideration
</Tip>

### Celebrate Completed Items

Keep recently completed items visible temporarily:
- Users see progress happening
- Builds confidence in your team
- Narrow your base filter to exclude old completed posts once they've been up for a while

### Communicate Changes

When plans change (and they will):
- Update the post's status or eta promptly, since the roadmap reflects it immediately
- Add comments to affected posts explaining why
- Notify subscribers of significant shifts

## Edit a roadmap

1. Hover over a roadmap in the sidebar
2. Click the **...** menu button
3. Select **Edit**
4. Update the name, layout, description, base filter, columns, or visibility
5. Click **Save**

## Delete a roadmap

1. Hover over a roadmap in the sidebar
2. Click the **...** menu button
3. Select **Delete**
4. Confirm deletion

<Note>
Deleting a roadmap only removes the saved view. The posts it showed are untouched because roadmap placement is derived from their fields, not stored on the roadmap.
</Note>

## Link from Your Product

Drive engagement by linking to your roadmap from your app:

- Footer links
- Help menu
- Feature request buttons

This turns your roadmap into a living communication channel with users.
